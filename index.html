How This Works

We have 7 content types. Some sell, some build community, some build trust. You don't post all 7 every week. You pick from the menu based on what's needed that week.

Content is NOT boutique-only. Some formats live in the boutique (The Runway, Real Talk). Others go wherever life happens: cafes, offices, parks, restaurants, the street. The clothes should be seen in the real world, not just on a rack.

These 7 types sit under broader content pillars: lifestyle, educational/value, brand storytelling, community, and social proof. Each content type maps to one or more of these pillars. Lifestyle content (outdoor shoots, cafe visits, real-world settings) runs through most of our types, not just one.

The only rule: The Runway posts every week. Everything else flexes around it.

Hero Piece of the Month

The strategy layer that runs through everything

Every month, pick 1-2 hero pieces from inventory. These get concentrated attention across multiple content types — The Runway, The Switch Up, Shop & Chat, and ad spend. Instead of spreading attention across 30 products, we make 1-2 pieces famous each month. By October, 4-6 pieces are "known."

Step 1
Pick the hero piece. Choose something versatile, photogenic, and mid-range in price. It should represent what Che Orika does best.
Step 2
Feature it across 3+ content types that month. It appears in The Runway, gets a One Piece Every Life video, and shows up in Shop & Chat features.
Step 3
The best-performing organic content featuring the hero piece becomes the paid ad creative. Concentrated budget, not scattered.
Step 4
Next month, new hero piece. The old one moves to supporting role. Over 3 months, 4-6 pieces become "known."
Content Type 01
The Che Orika Runway
Mini runway walk in the boutique. The anchor of the page.
Weekly (4-5/month)
Where
Boutique
This one stays in the boutique. Always. The boutique runway is the signature. A cleared walking path on the floor, 3-4 metres long. Someone walks toward the camera wearing a piece. Low camera angle (hip or knee height), natural daylight, confident walk. Each outfit gets a moment. Multiple outfits are cut together on beat. When people see this format, they know it's Che Orika.
How to Film
  • Camera: Phone on tripod, set low (hip height). Static. Never moves. Same angle every time.
  • Lighting: Natural daylight from boutique windows. No ring light.
  • The walk: Start at far end, walk toward camera, pause close, slow turn, walk back.
  • Editing: Cut outfit changes on the beat of the music. 3-5 outfits per video, 30-45 seconds.
  • Music: One consistent vibe across all Runway posts. Warm, mid-tempo, afrobeat-influenced. This sound becomes ours.
  • Who walks: Rotate. Cherechi, staff, customers, friends. Different women keep it fresh. Don't let it be the same 2-3 people every week.
  • Batchable: Film 4 weeks of Runway content in one afternoon.
On Screen
First 3 Seconds (Hook)
Text hook that stops the scroll. NOT the logo. The logo does not stop scrolls. The hook does.
Each Outfit
Piece name + price displayed on screen
End Frame
CTA: "DM your size" or "Tap to order"
Watermark
Small Che Orika logo, corner, throughout
Example Hooks
"Top 5 dresses under 50K"
"The piece every Abuja woman is asking about"
"Our top 3 shorts this month"
"5 pieces you can wear to court and to dinner"
"The 1 jacket you need in your wardrobe"
"Top 10 dresses in the store right now"
Why It Works
Batchable (film 4 weeks in one afternoon), every video doubles as an ad creative, and the hero piece should always appear in at least one Runway per month.

Content Type 02
Shop & Chat with Che Orika
Someone interesting comes to the boutique. They shop, they talk, we film.
2-4 / month
Where
Boutique
An interesting person comes to the boutique, shops, and chats. It's like filming two friends shopping and talking. The guest picks pieces, tries things on, talks about their life, their career, what they like. We film the whole thing and edit it down to 30-60 seconds. The funniest or most interesting moment becomes the hook.
How It Works
  • The vibe: Two friends shopping and talking. Not a formal interview. Natural, relaxed, funny.
  • While they shop: Ask about their life, their work, their hobbies. As they mention something (e.g., "I love clubbing"), we can cut to clips of that (from their own phone gallery) to give people a vivid picture.
  • The styling: As they talk about their lifestyle, the outfit choices they make start to make sense. A corporate woman picks the blazer dress. A creative picks the bold prints. The shopping reveals the person.
  • The hook: Whatever the funniest or most attention-grabbing moment is becomes the opening of the video. Not a formal intro.
  • Length: 30-60 seconds max. Gen Z has no attention span. Keep it tight.
Advanced Versions
Solo Guest
One person comes in, shops, chats. The standard format. Start here.
Two Guests, Two Lives
Bring two people with different lives (e.g., a content creator and a banker). They style each other from their own perspective. Ask the audience: who styled it better? What outfit do you like the most?
On Screen

CTA

Credit
Tag the guest's handle
Who to Bring
Not influencers or celebrities. Just interesting people with personality. Start with people you know. After a few episodes, ask the audience: "Who do you want to see shop with us next?"

Content Type 03
The Switch Up
One piece. Multiple ways. Watch the switch up.
2-3 / month
Where
Outdoor (Primary) Boutique (Fallback)
Take one piece. Style it three different ways for three different occasions. The default is to film each look in a real location: the office look is filmed walking into an actual office building, the brunch look is at an actual cafe, the dinner look is at an actual restaurant. This is "functional pieces for everywhere you go" as proof, not a promise. The boutique version (3 outfit changes in-store) works when time is short, but the outdoor version is stronger.
How to Film
  • Same piece, 3 different styling setups (change accessories, shoes, hair, bag)
  • Film each look in a different real-life location. A cafe. A corporate lobby. A dinner spot. A park.
  • Quick cuts between the 3 looks. 15-20 seconds total.
  • Works best with the hero piece of the month or mid-range versatile pieces you want to move
  • Fallback: film all 3 looks in the boutique with outfit changes. Still works, just less impactful.
On Screen
Piece Name + Price
Always visible. Always. No exceptions.
Occasion Labels
"To the office" / "To brunch" / "To a date"
Example Hooks
"One dress. Three lives. N38,000."
"She wore it to work, to dinner, and to church"
"Your friends will think you own three different dresses"
"N45,000. Monday to Sunday."
Why This Matters
Best format for moving inventory. Hero piece always gets a Switch Up video. Price always on screen. Every one of these is a product ad disguised as lifestyle content.

Content Type 04
Dates with Che Orika
A monthly community activity. Different every time.
1 / month
Where
Outdoor Rotating Venues Boutique (Sometimes)
Once a month, we bring 5-8 women together for an activity. The venue and activity rotate every time. A picnic in a park. A rooftop brunch. A paint-and-sip. A cooking class. A garden hangout. A day at a fashion workshop (learning to cut, bead-making, etc.). The common thread is Che Orika women doing life together, wearing the pieces. Every event is a content factory.
What It Produces
  • 6-8 outfit posts (each attendee in their styled look)
  • 2-3 reels (event montage, outfit reveals, candid moments)
  • 15-20 Stories (real-time coverage that creates FOMO for next month)
  • Attendees post their own photos = free reach to their networks
  • One event = roughly 2 weeks of content
Keep It Simple
Budget under N50,000 per event. Keep it simple. If it starts feeling like event management, scale it back.
First 3 Months
August: Picnic in the park
September: Rooftop brunch
October: Paint and sip
* Always credit. Every attendee is tagged in every piece of content they appear in. Feature their name, what they do, and their handle. They are collaborators, not background extras.

Content Type 05
Real Talk with Che Orika
Cherechi talks to camera. Opinions, tips, education, honest takes.
2-3 / month
Where
Boutique (Always)
Cherechi picks up her phone, stands in the boutique, and talks about something her customer cares about. The boutique is always the backdrop for this format. The space becomes part of the brand identity. People start associating the interior with Cherechi's voice.
Covers Two Things
Opinions, Hot Takes & Controversy
Things other brands won't say. "I'm going to be honest..." energy. Don't be afraid of controversy. Topics that make people argue in the comments drive massive reach. The ones that perform are the ones where Cherechi says something spicy, honest, or slightly provocative, something people screenshot and share.
Tips and Education
How to find your size for ready-to-wear. 3 fabrics that survive Abuja heat. What to wear to a Nigerian wedding that isn't aso ebi. Searchable content that brings new audiences.
How to Film
  • Always in the boutique. The space is part of the brand identity.
  • Phone in hand or on tripod. 30-45 seconds max.
  • No script. Just know the one point you want to make and say it.
  • Add captions in editing (most people watch without sound).
Example Topics
"How to find your size for ready-to-wear"
"Why your clothes don't last (and what to look for)"
"What nobody tells you about buying clothes online in Nigeria"
"3 fabrics that actually work in Abuja heat"
"The real cost of a well-made dress"

Content Type 06
Voice & Vibes
Voiceovers, trending audio, memes, BTS. The fun stuff.
Flexible (2-4 / month)
Where
Boutique Outdoor Anywhere
This is the category for everything that keeps the page alive and relatable. It can happen anywhere: the boutique, the fabric market, walking through Abuja, packing orders, wherever life happens. It's NOT the main content engine. It's the seasoning. Use it to fill gaps, ride trends when they're genuinely funny, and show the brand's personality.
The Different Types
Trending Audio
A trending sound paired with outfit transitions or styling moments. Quick, native to the platform, easy to produce.
Voiceovers
Original or trending voiceover laid over outfit footage. "When your friend says she has nothing to wear but her wardrobe is full of Che Orika..."
Memes and Humour
Relatable fashion humour. Not random. Has to connect to the brand or the customer's life. "POV: You said you're done buying clothes this month..."
Behind the Scenes
Packing orders, new stock arriving, fabric shopping at the market, a funny moment in the store, setting up for a shoot. Raw, unfiltered. Can be outdoor or in-store.
The Rule
If you only have energy for 3 posts this week, none of them should be Voice & Vibes. The Runway, Shop & Chat, The Switch Up, and Real Talk come first.

Content Type 07
Spotted in Che Orika
Customer reposts. Orika Sisters wearing the pieces in the wild.
Ongoing
Where
Wherever They Are
When a customer wears a Che Orika piece and posts about it, we repost. We create a culture where Orika Sisters tag us, and we celebrate them. This is social proof. It shows potential customers that real people buy from us and love what they got. It costs nothing and grows on its own over time.
How It Works
  • Check tags and DMs daily for customer photos
  • Repost to Stories immediately (with credit and a tag)
  • Best ones go to the main feed as a proper post. If the customer sent a verified/edited picture, post it directly without overthinking.
  • Signature sound: Use the XOXO Gossip Girl sound as the signature audio for Spotted posts. When people hear it, they know it's a customer feature.
  • Caption always credits the customer: name, handle, what she's wearing, piece name and price
  • Encourage tagging: "Tag us when you wear your Che Orika piece and you might be featured"
* Always ask permission before reposting to the main feed. Stories reposts are standard, but a feed feature should feel like a collaboration, not a surprise.

The 3-Month Roadmap

August to establish the system. September to refine. October to run. Then we reassess everything.

August
Build the Foundation
Hero Piece
Pick 1 hero piece. Feature it across at least 3 content types. Learn what resonates.
The Runway
Film test episode. Lock the format: camera angle, music, edit style. Start posting weekly.
Shop & Chat
First guest. Someone interesting, not necessarily an influencer. Film the shopping and chatting experience. Test the format.
The Switch Up
2 videos. Can start in-boutique while we figure out outdoor location logistics.
The Edit
First event: Picnic in the park. Small, 6-8 women. Film everything.
Real Talk
2-3 videos. Test which topics get saves and shares. Learn what Cherechi's audience responds to.
Voice & Vibes
Fill gaps. 2-4 posts. Include outdoor BTS.
Goal
Establish the system. Every content type gets tested at least once. Find what works.
September
Refine and Expand
Hero Piece
Pick 1-2 new hero pieces. August's hero should now have social proof (customer photos, DMs, reposts).
The Runway
Weekly. Consistent. Rotate who walks. The format should feel locked in and recognizable.
Shop & Chat
2-4 episodes. Ask audience: "Should we make this a series?" Try the two-guest format (e.g., content creator + banker styling each other).
The Switch Up
2-3 videos. At least 1 fully outdoor (cafe, office, dinner). Test how outdoor performs vs. boutique.
The Edit
Second event: Rooftop brunch. Different activity, same community energy.
Real Talk
2-3 videos. Double down on the topics that got saves in August. Cut the ones that didn't.
Voice & Vibes
2-4 posts. Only the ones that genuinely fit.
Goal
Refine what works, cut what doesn't. First outdoor One Piece shoot. Community starts recognizing the formats.
October
The System is Running
Hero Piece
1-2 hero pieces. By now we know what sells. Hero selection is data-informed, not guesswork.
The Runway
Weekly. Consider a "best of" compilation reel at end of month. The format is now a known brand signature.
Shop & Chat
2-4 episodes. By now people are asking to be on it. The series has its own momentum.
The Switch Up
2-3 videos. Mostly outdoor. This is now a proven format. Use it to move inventory intentionally.
The Edit
Third event: Paint and sip. By now there's a waitlist. The event is becoming a thing people talk about.
Real Talk
2-3 videos. Cherechi is becoming the voice people trust on fashion in Abuja.
Voice & Vibes
2-4 posts. The seasoning, never the meal.
Goal
System running smoothly. Assess everything. What do we keep, what do we change, what do we scale for Q4?

How We Track What's Working

Every two weeks, review the numbers that matter. Not likes. Saves, shares, DMs, and sales.

Content Metrics (Every 2 Weeks)
Saves: How many people saved each post (signals purchase intent)
Shares: How many people sent it to a friend
DMs generated: Which posts led to inquiries
Follower growth: Net new followers in the period
Top 3 posts: Which content types performed best
Sales Metrics (Every 2 Weeks)
DM inquiries: Total number of people who messaged about buying
DM to sale conversion: How many inquiries became purchases
Revenue from content: Which posts led to actual sales
Hero piece performance: Did the hero piece sell? How many?
Average response time: How fast are we replying to DMs?
DM response target: under 30 minutes during business hours. Data shows this triples the conversion rate (59% vs 20% for slow replies). This is the single highest-impact thing we can do. Fast replies = more sales from the same content.

Filming and Batching

Two filming days per month covers 80% of content. Batch it, don't create one-by-one.

Filming Day 1
The Runway: Film 4-5 Runway videos in one afternoon. Set up tripod, line up outfits, walk and change. 3 hours total.

Output: One full month of Runway content done.
Filming Day 2
The Switch Up: Film 2-3 videos (outdoor or in-boutique).
Real Talk: Film 2-3 talking-to-camera videos back to back.

Output: One month of One Piece + Real Talk done.
The Edit Event
Monthly event: Produces its own content burst. 6-8 outfit posts + 2-3 reels + Stories.

Output: Roughly 2 weeks of content from one afternoon.
Shop & Chat can't be fully batched (depends on other people's schedules). Schedule 2-3 women per month to come to the boutique for styling. Voice & Vibes and Spotted are spontaneous, they happen as they happen. Editing: Use CapCut (free). Auto-captions on everything. Same colour filter on all Runway videos for consistency.

Always

  • Use Rouge as the primary accent on every touchpoint
  • Use Espresso instead of pure black everywhere
  • Use Ivory as default background — never stark white
  • Use Blush for product photography backdrops
  • Use Gold sparingly — "Best Seller" and special moments only
  • Include CMYK values when sending to printers

Never

  • Use pure black (#000000) anywhere
  • Use pure white (#FFFFFF) as a background
  • Use Rouge as a full-page background (newsletter is the ONLY exception)
  • Use Gold as a primary CTA colour
  • Add new colours without approval from Creative Director
  • Use gradients on the brand colours

Key rule: Playfair Display is for DISPLAY only — headlines, logo, editorial moments. Never for body text, never for small UI. Inter handles everything else. The contrast between a decorative serif (Playfair) and a clean sans (Inter) is what creates the "luxury but accessible" tension.

Logo Rules

  • Always Playfair Display Regular + Italic
  • "Ché" matches the background's text colour
  • "Orika" is always Rouge (#A91C2A) + italic
  • On Rouge background, both are white
  • Minimum clear space = height of the "C" on all sides

Never

  • Make the logo bold or semibold
  • Set the logo in ALL CAPS
  • Change the spacing between Ché and Orika
  • Use a different colour for "Orika" (always rouge)
  • Add effects — no shadows, no outlines, no gradients

07 — Photography Direction
How the Clothes Come Alive

The Ché Orika Look

  • Warm natural light — golden hour or soft indoor
  • Women in movement — walking, turning, adjusting
  • Candid energy — like a friend took the photo
  • Real skin, real textures — minimal retouching
  • Backgrounds: blush/ivory backdrops OR real locations (boutique, streets, interiors)
  • Film-grain quality — slightly warm colour grade
  • Show the clothes ALIVE — fabric moving, draping, catching light

Never

  • Harsh flash or cold studio lighting
  • Stiff posed photography — hands at sides, dead stare
  • Heavy retouching — smoothed skin, warped proportions
  • White seamless backgrounds — too clinical
  • Stock photography of non-African women
  • Heavy filters or presets that change skin tone
  • Flat lays — show clothes on bodies, not on tables
08 — Brand Voice
How We Sound

We Sound Like

  • A friend who has taste — warm, knowing, never preachy
  • Confident without being loud
  • Short sentences. No filler. Every word earns its place.
  • First line is always the hook — stop the scroll
  • "She wore it like she meant it." — that energy
  • Personal — like one woman talking to another

We Never Sound Like

  • "Excited to announce..." — that's a press release
  • "Link in bio!!!! 🔥🔥🔥" — that's an Instagram vendor
  • Corporate jargon — "leveraging synergies"
  • Desperate — "DM to order NOW before it sells out!!!"
  • Generic — "fashion for every woman" (we are specific)
  • Apologetic — "sorry for the late post" (we don't apologise for being a brand)
